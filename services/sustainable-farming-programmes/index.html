Sustainable farming programmes beyond cotton

Mango branch in an orchard in Sadiqabad, Punjab

01 / 03

We design and run organic, regenerative and good-agricultural-practice programmes for food and flower crops. The method is the one we use in cotton: organise, train, inspect, certify and link to market. We adapt it to each crop's season and to what its buyers require.

Why it matters for exporters

Export buyers increasingly set conditions before price: GlobalG.A.P. certification, residue limits, aflatoxin limits, and now regenerative claims. A rejected consignment costs more than a programme. We help growers meet those conditions on the farm, where most problems start.

What we do

  • Assess the farm or groupPractices, inputs, records and the gaps against the standard your buyer asks for
  • Plan the changeA season-by-season plan the grower can actually follow
  • Train and supportIn the orchard or field, at the right time of year
  • Inspect and documentSo the farm is ready when the auditor comes
  • Link to buyers or processingWhere it helps the grower earn more (see value addition)

Questions growers ask

Can a mango or date orchard be certified regenerative?

Yes. Regenerative standards cover perennial crops as well as field crops. We began regenerative certification work with a mango orchard in 2025, and the Good Dates Platform starts in 2026.

What does GlobalG.A.P. involve?

It's a farm assurance standard covering food safety, traceability, the environment and worker welfare. Farms need documented procedures, input records and hygiene controls. We help put them in place and keep them running.

Do you work with single farms or only groups?

Both. Some projects have one grower (the mango orchard has one owner); others have hundreds of farmers (610 in the Good Dates Platform).

Need a farm ready for GlobalG.A.P. or regenerative certification?
